# IPC-2221 trace heating: the formula `pcb.net_width` uses

**Source.** IPC-2221 (Generic Standard on Printed Board Design, revision B, 2012),
section 6.2, "Conductive Material Requirements", and its conductor-sizing chart.
The chart dates from NBS measurements in the 1950s; the closed form below is the
curve fit to it that IPC-2221 and every trace-width calculator since use.

## The formula

    I  = k * dT^0.44 * A^0.725
    dT = (I / (k * A^0.725))^(1 / 0.44)

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| I | steady current through the track | A |
| dT | temperature rise of the track above ambient | C |
| A | cross-section: width x thickness | mil^2 |
| k | 0.048 for an external (outer-layer) track, 0.024 for an internal one | |

Thickness from copper weight: 1 oz/ft^2 = 1.378 mil = 35 um. Width in mm divides by
0.0254 to give mil.

## Worked check (the baseline board)

VBUS at 0.5 A on a 0.25 mm outer track, 1 oz:

- width 0.25 / 0.0254 = 9.843 mil, thickness 1.378 mil, A = 13.56 mil^2
- A^0.725 = 6.62, k A^0.725 = 0.048 x 6.62 = 0.318
- dT = (0.5 / 0.318)^(2.273) = 1.573^2.273 = **2.8 C**

The known-bad control narrows VBUS to the width that puts the same current at
11.5 C (15% past the 10 C limit): 0.1061 mm.

## Fitted range

The chart covers 0 to 35 A, widths up to 400 mil (10.2 mm), rises of 10 to 100 C,
and 0.5 to 3 oz copper. Outside it the formula is extrapolation, and the verdict
says which input is outside. A 10 C limit is at the edge of the range, on purpose:
it is the common design choice and the lowest rise the chart supports.

## Why IPC-2221 and not IPC-2152

IPC-2152 (2009) replaced the chart with new measurements and is more accurate: the
old chart treats internal tracks as twice as bad as external ones, which IPC-2152
found pessimistic, and it ignores board thickness, nearby planes and mounting.
*Rejected* for this gate because IPC-2152 is a family of charts with correction
factors and no closed form a reader can check by hand, and because the 2221 fit is
conservative against it for the common case. A project that needs the tighter
answer states its own limit, or adds a gate that cites IPC-2152's charts.

## What the formula does not cover

- Vias: a via's barrel is thin plating, often the narrowest point of a power path.
- Pads and pours: wider than any track, not judged.
- Pulsed current: the formula is for steady state. A motor's stall current for one
  second heats a track far less than the formula says; continuous current is what
  it answers.
- Voltage drop: a track can be cool and still drop too much voltage. That is
  R = rho L / A, a different claim.
